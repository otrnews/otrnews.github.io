Most owner-operators got into trucking to haul freight, not to chase invoices, sort receipts, and dig through email for a rate confirmation. But the business side decides whether the truck makes money. Zoho is a set of business apps that can handle much of that work from one account, and it is priced for small companies.

This guide walks through which Zoho apps fit a trucking business, how a one-truck operation and a small fleet might use them, what it costs, and where you still need other tools.

## What Zoho is

Zoho is a software company that makes dozens of business apps: accounting, expense tracking, customer management, e-signatures, forms, file storage, and more. You can buy apps one at a time, or get most of them together in a bundle called **Zoho One**, which includes more than 45 apps under one subscription.

The apps share the same login and talk to each other. A broker you add in one app shows up when you create an invoice in another, and a signed document can land in the same file folder as the load it belongs to.

## The apps that matter most for trucking

### Zoho Books for invoicing and bookkeeping
Zoho Books is the accounting app. For a trucking business, that means:

- **Invoices from your phone** right after delivery, with the rate confirmation and bill of lading attached.
- **Payment reminders** that go out automatically when a broker is late, so you are not the one chasing them.
- **Bank and card feeds** that pull in transactions so fuel, tolls, repairs, and insurance get categorized instead of piling up.
- **Reports** that show profit, what each customer owes you, and what you spent by category, which makes quarterly tax estimates and tax season much less painful. Your accountant can be given access directly.

### Zoho Expense for receipts
Fuel, scales, tolls, lumpers, parking, and repairs create a pile of paper receipts. Zoho Expense lets you snap a photo of a receipt and pulls out the amount, date, and vendor. For a fleet, drivers can submit their own expenses from the road, and you approve them from your phone.

### Zoho CRM for brokers and shippers
Good carriers keep track of who they work with. A CRM (customer relationship manager) holds every broker and shipper contact, the lanes they post, the rates they paid, notes from calls, and reminders to follow up. Over time that becomes a list of customers who pay well and pay fast, which is how carriers work their way toward direct freight.

### Zoho Sign for paperwork
Driver agreements, lease agreements, and other documents can be sent and signed on a phone. No printing, scanning, or waiting for a fax.

### Zoho WorkDrive for documents
Keep insurance certificates, permits, registrations, medical cards, and load paperwork in organized folders you can pull up on your phone at a customer or during an audit.

### Zoho Forms and Zoho Creator for your own checklists
Zoho Forms can turn driver applications or internal checklists into phone-friendly forms. Zoho Creator lets you build simple custom apps, like a maintenance tracker that records oil changes, tires, and repairs by truck and reminds you when service is due.

## How it looks for a one-truck owner-operator

A typical week might run like this:

- Deliver the load, open Zoho Books on your phone, and send the invoice with the paperwork attached before you pull out of the lot.
- Snap receipts for fuel and tolls as you go, so nothing ends up in a shoebox.
- Log the broker's contact and the rate in the CRM so you know who to call next time you are in that lane.
- At the end of the month, check the profit report to see what the truck actually earned after every expense.

If you factor your invoices, keep a record in Zoho Books of what was factored and what fees were taken, so your books match what actually hit your bank account.

## How it looks for a small fleet

Once you have drivers, the value grows:

- Drivers submit receipts and expenses from the road, and you approve them in one place.
- Driver files, including applications, agreements, and medical card dates, stay organized and easy to find.
- Everyone works from the same customer list, so a dispatcher knows the history with each broker.
- Dashboards show revenue and costs by truck, which tells you which trucks and lanes earn the most.

## What it costs

Zoho sells most apps on their own, often with low-cost starting plans, and some offer free versions with limits. **Zoho One** bundles everything and has two pricing models: one where you license every employee at a lower per-person price, and one where you license only the people who use it at a higher per-user price. Recent published pricing starts around $37 per employee per month on annual billing for the all-employee plan, with higher rates for monthly billing and for the per-user plan.

A simple rule of thumb: if you only need invoicing and receipts, start with Zoho Books and Zoho Expense on their own. If you will use four or more apps across a team, Zoho One usually costs less than buying them separately. Check Zoho's current pricing page before you decide, because plans and prices change.

## Where Zoho falls short

Zoho runs the business side, not the truck. It is not:

- **An ELD.** You still need a registered electronic logging device for hours of service.
- **A load board or TMS.** It will not find freight or dispatch loads the way a load board or transportation management system does.
- **Factoring.** It tracks invoices but does not advance you money on them.
- **IFTA and permit filing.** You can keep records in it, but you will likely still need a dedicated service or your accountant for fuel-tax filings.

Most carriers pair Zoho with a load board, an ELD, and, if needed, a factoring company.

## Getting started

- Start with the two biggest headaches, usually invoicing and receipts, and set those up first.
- Connect your business bank account and card so transactions flow in automatically.
- Add your regular brokers and shippers as customers.
- Invite your accountant so they can pull what they need at tax time.
- Add more apps only as you need them.

**Bottom line:** If paperwork and invoicing are eating your evenings, Zoho can pull the business side of a trucking company into one place for a fraction of what separate tools cost. Start small, and add apps as your fleet grows.
