Trucking job ads are written to get a call, so they lead with the biggest number they can. The real job is in the details. Here's how to read an ad and what to ask before you sign anything.

## Understand how you'll be paid

- **Cents per mile (CPM).** Ask whether pay is based on practical miles or shortest-route miles, since shortest-route miles often pay you for fewer miles than you drive.
- **Percentage of the load.** Common for owner-operators and some company jobs. Ask what the percentage is taken from, and whether fuel surcharge is included.
- **Hourly.** Common for local work. Ask about overtime and guaranteed hours.
- **Extras.** Detention, layover, breakdown pay, and stop pay add up. Ask what each pays and when it starts.

## Read the pay claim closely

"Up to" means the top, not the typical. Ask what an average driver in that position actually made last year, and how many miles they ran. A weekly minimum or guaranteed pay tells you more than a yearly maximum.

## Pin down home time

"Home weekly" can mean 34 hours or two full days. Ask how many nights a week you'll be home, which days, and how often drivers actually get home on time.

## Know what you're agreeing to

- **Sign-on bonus.** Find out how it's paid. Many are spread out over months and require you to stay.
- **Training contracts.** Company-paid CDL training often requires you to stay for a set time or repay the cost. Get the terms in writing.
- **Lease-purchase offers.** Ask who sets your loads, what happens if you quit early, and what you still owe. Some drivers walk away owing money and without a truck.
- **Equipment.** Ask the age of the trucks, whether you get an assigned truck, and what the cabs are equipped with.

## Red flags

- The recruiter can't say what an average driver earns.
- Pay terms are vague or change between calls.
- You're pushed to sign the same day.
- Current drivers say home time or miles don't match the ad.

Ask to talk to a current driver. A good company will let you.

**Bottom line:** Treat a job ad as a starting point. Get pay, home time, and any contract terms in writing before you commit.
