A breakdown costs money, but a truck stopped on the shoulder is also a crash risk. What you do in the first ten minutes matters most.

## Get off the road safely

- Signal early and get as far onto the shoulder as you can, ideally on a straight stretch where other drivers can see you.
- Avoid stopping on curves, hills, bridges, or in construction zones if the truck can make it a little farther.
- Turn on your four-way flashers right away.

## Set out your warning devices

Federal rules require you to put out warning triangles within 10 minutes of stopping. Where they go depends on the road:

- **Two-way road.** One triangle about 10 feet from the truck on the traffic side, one about 100 feet behind, and one about 100 feet ahead.
- **Divided highway or one-way road.** Place them about 10 feet, 100 feet, and 200 feet behind the truck, toward approaching traffic.
- **Hills, curves, or obstructions.** Move the rear triangle back farther, up to 500 feet, so drivers see it in time.

Wear a reflective vest, walk facing traffic, and stay off the travel lanes.

## Figure out what's wrong

Check warning lights and fault codes, look for leaks, and note what happened just before the problem. Write down your exact location using mile markers or exits. Good details help a road service tech bring the right parts the first time.

## Get help

- **Company drivers.** Call your fleet's breakdown line and follow its process.
- **Owner-operators.** Call your roadside service or a mobile repair shop. Get a price and a time estimate before work starts, and ask for an itemized invoice.
- If the truck blocks traffic or anyone is hurt, call 911 first.

## Protect your load and your logbook

Tell your broker or dispatcher right away, since late deliveries hurt less when they're reported early. Log your time correctly. Waiting for repairs is generally on-duty, not-driving time unless you're relieved of duty.

## Prevent the next one

Most breakdowns start as something small: a worn belt, an underinflated tire, a leaking air line. A thorough pre-trip and a set maintenance schedule catch most of them before they strand you.

**Bottom line:** Get off the road, get your triangles out within 10 minutes, and give your repair service clear details. A roadside plan in place before you need it saves hours when something goes wrong.
