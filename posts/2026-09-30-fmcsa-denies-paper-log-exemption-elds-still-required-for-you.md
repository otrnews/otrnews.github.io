FMCSA has denied a request that would have let drivers go back to paper logbooks. If you're required to keep records of duty status, you still need a compliant ELD. Nothing changes on the road.

## What happened

On September 29, FMCSA announced it was denying an exemption application from the Federation of Professional Truckers (FOPT). FOPT is a driver-led group formed about a year ago. The request went beyond the group's own members. It would have let any driver or motor carrier choose a paper logbook.

Today, paper logs are allowed only for drivers who use them eight days or fewer in a 30-day period, or for trucks with engines built before 2000.

FOPT made three main arguments. Paper logs are still enforceable, and officers nationwide understand them. Small carriers also carry a disproportionate financial burden from ELDs. The petition said ELD compliance costs more than $500 a year per truck, citing an FMCSA impact analysis. The group also pointed to ELD malfunctions.

## Why FMCSA said no

The agency gave two reasons, according to Overdrive's summary of the decision.

**It says its hands are tied.** FMCSA said the MAP-21 highway bill requires ELDs for commercial vehicles in interstate commerce. It said it "cannot grant a blanket exemption" for any driver or carrier that wants out.

**It didn't see a safety case.** FMCSA found that FOPT's application didn't explain how the exemption would be as safe as, or safer than, the current rules. The agency also said the group's safeguards were built around its members. The exemption would have covered anyone who wanted it, and FOPT didn't explain how its safeguards would apply to an unknown group of drivers.

Drivers weighed in heavily. The request drew more than 900 comments on the Federal Register, and most supported it. Opponents included the American Trucking Associations, the Truck Safety Coalition, Road Safe America and the Institute for Safer Trucking.

## What this means for small carriers

This is the latest in a long line of denials. FMCSA turned down OOIDA's request for a five-year small-business exemption in 2018. In 2024 it denied a one-truck owner-operator's request. It said economic difficulties don't justify an exemption.

The timing is bad for cash flow. Diesel prices are near record high. Any relief on fixed costs would have helped. This denial closes off one route for it.

The ELD cost is real, but the alternatives aren't free. Paper logs mean more time on paperwork, and roadside inspectors would still check them. Under the current rules, a driver who used paper logs outside the narrow exceptions would risk a violation.

## What to do

- **Keep your ELD registered and working.** Make sure it's on FMCSA's registered device list, and keep the instruction sheet and malfunction procedures in the cab.
- **Know the paper-log exceptions.** The eight-days-in-30 rule and the pre-2000 engine rule are the only routine exceptions. Don't assume a paper log is legal outside them.
- **Know the malfunction rule.** FMCSA's malfunction procedures already allow temporary paper logs when an ELD isn't working. That comes from a safety group's comments, so read the actual rule before relying on it. Keep blank log sheets on hand.
- **Shop your ELD.** Opponents said ELD costs have dropped a lot, and many low-cost compliant devices are available. That claim comes from opponents of the exemption. Compare monthly fees and contract terms before renewing.
- **Comment on future petitions.** Exemption requests are public. Comments don't override the statute, but they are the way to be heard.

**Bottom line:** FMCSA says only Congress can drop the ELD mandate, so keep a working, registered ELD in every truck.
