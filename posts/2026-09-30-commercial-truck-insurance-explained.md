Insurance is one of the largest costs of running your own truck, and getting it wrong can cost your authority or your business. Here's what each coverage does and which ones you actually need.

## What the law requires

- **Primary auto liability.** Covers injuries and damage you cause to others. For-hire carriers hauling general freight interstate in trucks over 10,001 pounds need at least $750,000 in coverage under federal rules. Hazardous materials require more. Many brokers and shippers want $1 million.
- **Your insurer's filing.** Your insurance company files proof of liability coverage with FMCSA. Your authority won't become active without it.

## What brokers and shippers usually require

- **Motor truck cargo.** Covers the freight you're hauling. It isn't federally required for most carriers, but many brokers require $100,000 or more before they'll give you a load.
- **General liability.** Covers incidents off the road, such as damage at a customer's dock. Many shippers and facilities ask for it.

## Coverages that protect you

- **Physical damage.** Covers your own truck and trailer after a crash, theft, fire, or storm. Your lender will almost certainly require it if you're financing the truck.
- **Non-trucking liability (bobtail).** For owner-operators leased to a carrier, it covers you when you're driving the truck but not under dispatch. It doesn't replace the carrier's primary liability.
- **Occupational accident.** Owner-operators usually aren't covered by workers' compensation. This coverage pays medical costs and lost income if you're hurt on the job. Some carriers require it for leased drivers.

## Why it costs so much

New authorities pay the most because insurers have no history to price from. Your driving record, the age and value of your equipment, the type of freight, and the states you run through all affect the price.

## Shopping tips

- Work with an agent who specializes in trucking, since general agents often don't have access to the right companies.
- Compare deductibles and exclusions, not just the price.
- Keep a clean record and good safety scores. Both help at renewal.
- Ask whether a safety course or dash camera lowers your premium.

**Bottom line:** Get the coverage the law requires, add what your brokers and lender require, and protect yourself with physical damage and occupational accident coverage. Compare the details before you compare prices.

This is general information, not insurance advice. Coverage requirements vary by operation, freight, and state, so confirm what you need with a licensed agent.
