If you've spent hours fighting FMCSA's new registration system this summer, you're not alone, and the agency has admitted it. FMCSA has temporarily stopped deactivating USDOT numbers for carriers who miss their biennial update, giving everyone breathing room while it works the bugs out of Motus.

That's good news for small fleets and owner-operators. But it isn't a free pass, and it doesn't protect you from every Motus problem. Here's what's going on and what to do about it.

## What changed

Motus, FMCSA's new USDOT registration system, went live on May 19, 2026, replacing the old registration portals. It's meant to make it harder for chameleon carriers and fraudsters to register, but the rollout has been rough, with widespread complaints about login trouble and records showing the wrong status.

In response, FMCSA suspended enforcement of the biennial update (the MCS-150 you file every two years). If your update came due on or after June 1, 2026, the agency says you'll get extra time and your USDOT number won't be inactivated just because the update wasn't filed.

FMCSA hasn't said when enforcement will restart. It has said carriers will get advance notice before it does.

## What it doesn't cover

The pause is narrow. It only protects you from deactivation for a missed biennial update. The requirement itself still exists, and you'll need to file once things settle down.

It also doesn't stop Motus from getting your record wrong. Last week, a one-truck carrier in Houston, Savoy Transportation, told a local TV station its authority had been wrongly shown as "inactive" for nearly two months. The owner said she contacted FMCSA more than 20 times with no fix. The status was corrected within about a day of the story airing, according to CDLLife. For a small carrier, two months without loads can be the end of the business.

## What to do this week

- **Check your status yourself.** Use the public search in Motus to look up your USDOT number and make sure it shows active, with the right company details. Don't assume.
- **Claim your Motus profile if you haven't.** Access runs through Login.gov with identity verification. If you already had a USDOT number, use the same Login.gov email that was listed for the company official in the old FMCSA portal.
- **Double-check your insurance and authority.** Records moved into Motus can carry outdated statuses. If something looks off, fix it before a broker or shipper notices.
- **File your biennial update when you can.** It's free, and you don't need a third-party service to do it, whatever the emails in your inbox say.
- **Keep a paper trail.** If you call or submit a ticket, write down the date, the reference number, and who you spoke with. Screenshot your status. If you lose loads over a glitch, that record matters.

## Where to get help

FMCSA's Registration Customer Service Center is at 1-800-832-5660, or you can submit a ticket through the agency's online help form at ask.fmcsa.dot.gov.

**Bottom line:** your USDOT number won't be shut off over a late biennial update right now, but check your record today. The carriers getting hurt aren't the ones who missed a filing. They're the ones who didn't know Motus had them listed wrong.
