Ohio will cut its diesel tax from 47 cents a gallon to 1 cent for 90 days. Gov. Mike DeWine signed the bill Thursday, Oct. 1, and it lowers the state's gasoline and diesel taxes to 1 cent per gallon. For a truck running through Ohio, that is the largest state-level diesel break reported so far this fall. It comes as diesel sits within a few cents of its all-time high.

## What Ohio's change means at the pump

Fox 8 reported the reduction starts Sunday, Oct. 4, and runs 90 days, to around the new year. Earlier coverage said the holiday would start as soon as the governor signed. Check the posted price at the pump before you count on the lower rate.

Rough math: the cut is about 46 cents a gallon. On a 150-gallon fill-up that is roughly $69, if the full amount reaches the pump price.

It may not. The bill bars licensed fuel dealers from keeping the savings and gives the Attorney General sole enforcement authority. Retailers can apply for refunds on tax already paid on fuel in their inventory. A retailer who paid the old tax on the fuel in its tanks may take a few days to lower prices. A spokesperson for the National Association of Convenience Stores said in the AP report that the effect is "way more complicated" than a simple drop at every pump.

Drivers will still pay the federal tax. That is separate from the state tax.

Fleets that file IFTA should check the Q4 Ohio rate when the quarterly rates are published. The reporting reviewed did not say how the holiday will be handled for IFTA filers.

## Other states

An Associated Press review found about one-third of states have some form of fuel tax relief, with many acting in the past week. The rules differ by state:

- **Indiana:** Gov. Mike Braun renewed the state's gas tax suspension from Oct. 6 through Nov. 4. Whether it covers diesel is unclear. Braun said in April that his first suspension did not apply to diesel, and the reports on the latest extension talk about gas taxes. Diesel hit a record $6.92 average in Indiana in September. Do not assume a break on diesel there.
- **Utah:** A 6-cent fuel tax reduction is in effect through the end of this year.
- **Illinois and Kentucky:** They have smaller breaks that delay increases that were due in July.

## Dyed diesel waivers are not for over-the-road trucks

Governors in Alabama, Arkansas, Louisiana, Missouri, Nebraska, North Carolina, North Dakota, Oklahoma and Texas have issued orders for harvest season. The orders target dyed diesel, which is exempt from state tax and normally limited to off-road equipment. The reporting describes these as relief for farmers, loggers and similar users. Nothing in it extends the waivers to other drivers. If you run for-hire freight, keep dyed fuel out of your tanks. Those rules are still enforced, and a roadside fuel check can lead to fines.

## Where prices stand

AAA put the national diesel average at $6.41 on Wednesday, a few cents shy of a record set last week. The record was $6.53 on Sept. 22. Tax holidays do not change the underlying market. They also end on a set date, and the AP report quoted an analyst saying that prices spring back as soon as collections restart.

## Fuel haulers get a separate break

FMCSA has a separate hours-of-service waiver for people hauling fuel. It waives HOS requirements for interstate gasoline and diesel haulers through Dec. 16. It allows up to 16 hours of driving in a 24-hour period, with at least a 6-hour sleeper berth break. Drivers must carry a copy of the waiver. Carriers with a conditional safety rating are not eligible. The waiver applies only to hauling gasoline and diesel. Other freight is not covered.

## What to do

- **Plan fuel stops around Ohio's holiday.** If you cross the state, check the price at stations before you pass through rather than relying on an old fuel card routine. Compare Ohio against Pennsylvania, Michigan and Indiana. Check whether your route's stations have dropped prices yet.
- **Save your receipts and gallon counts.** You will need them for IFTA. Ohio's gallons will carry a much lower tax rate for the holiday period if the rate is published that way.
- **Look at your fuel surcharge.** If you are an owner-operator on a contract with a surcharge, find out whether your broker or shipper bases it on a national average. A state tax holiday will not change that average much.
- **Know when each break ends.** Ohio's runs about 90 days. Indiana's current order ends Nov. 4 and will be reviewed again. Expect prices to jump back when a holiday ends.
- **Do not run dyed diesel in a for-hire truck.** The harvest-season orders are for ag and timber use.

**Bottom line:** Ohio's 46-cent diesel cut is real money for trucks that fuel there, but check the price at the pump, keep clean IFTA records and don't count on relief in every state.
