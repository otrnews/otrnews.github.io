The renewal season for 2027 permits opened Oct. 1, and the Unified Carrier Registration (UCR) fee costs more this time. FMCSA's final rule raises annual UCR fees by an average of 20 percent, starting with the 2027 registration year. Every owner-operator who runs interstate needs to budget for it and file before the year ends.

## What changed on UCR

FMCSA published the final rule on Sept. 1. The rule took effect Oct. 1, 2026. Increases range from $9 to $9,329 per entity, depending on fleet-size bracket.

Based on the fee table in the rule as reproduced by one compliance firm, the 2027 fees by fleet size are:

- 0–2 vehicles: $55, up from $46
- 3–5 vehicles: $167, up from $138
- 6–20 vehicles: $333, up from $276
- 21–100 vehicles: $1,163, up from $963

For a single-truck owner-operator, the increase is $9. For a small fleet, it adds up across several units.

FMCSA said it has no room to drop the fee. The agency said Congress set up the UCR plan in statute, so neither FMCSA nor the UCR Board can stop collecting fees. The agency also noted that the 2027 fees are still below what carriers paid from 2019 through 2022.

## Who has to file, and when

UCR is an annual permit that most motor carriers pay if they have an active U.S. DOT number marked interstate. The permit has to be kept up whether or not a driver is actually using that DOT number.

The window to file and pay is open now and runs through Dec. 31. Carriers that are already registered can usually finish the process online. Filing services say enforcement can begin Jan. 1. That date comes from those services, not from FMCSA's rule.

Carriers based in a state that does not take part in UCR are not off the hook. The non-participating states are Arizona, Florida, Hawaii, Maryland, Nevada, New Jersey, Oregon, Vermont and Wyoming, and Washington, D.C. also does not participate. One filing guide says carriers based in those places still register, through a participating base state. Check with your base state before you pay.

## IFTA and other renewals

Oct. 1 also opened the renewal window for IFTA and weight/distance permits. Rules differ by state. Some states auto-renew IFTA permits. Others make you do the work, and most of those mail a reminder.

Ohio shows how it can go. Ohio requires an approved IFTA renewal by Dec. 31, and the renewal period begins Oct. 1. Ohio mails the new license and decals in December, but only to licensees whose renewals were approved. If you wait until mid-December, a mailing delay could leave you running without current decals.

Apportioned plate renewals bring their own paperwork. Oregon, for example, requires proof of the Heavy Vehicle Use Tax, a receipted Schedule 1 for the tax period of July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2027, for vehicles registered at 55,000 pounds or more. The Form 2290 deadline for vehicles in service at the start of that period was Aug. 31, and later-added vehicles follow their own deadlines. If you have not filed yet, do it now. Your DMV or IRP office will want the stamped Schedule 1.

## What to do

- **Count your trucks.** Your UCR bracket depends on how many commercial vehicles you own or operate. Make sure the number matches your records before you pay.
- **File UCR through the official site.** The UCR Plan runs the registration system at plan.ucr.gov. For questions, the plan lists a phone line, 1-833-UCR-PLAN (1-833-827-7526). Filing services charge extra on top of the government fee, so compare before you pay one.
- **Look up your state's IFTA and IRP rules.** Find out whether your state auto-renews or wants an application. Note the due date.
- **Confirm your 2290 is done.** Keep the stamped Schedule 1 in your renewal folder.
- **Set aside the cash now.** A year-end pile of renewals, fees and taxes can hit when freight is slow and fuel is still expensive.
- **Do not wait for the last week.** Fixing a bad fleet count or a failed payment takes time. The December holidays slow down offices and the mail.

**Bottom line:** UCR costs about 20% more for 2027, the Dec. 31 deadline is firm, and IFTA and plate renewals are due on the same calendar, so file in October or November.
