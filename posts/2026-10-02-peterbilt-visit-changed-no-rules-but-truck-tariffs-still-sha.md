If you are thinking about buying a new truck, the President's Peterbilt visit is mostly noise. It does not change your price or your options. The tariff and emissions rules that do affect your costs were already in place, and one of them is still a proposal.

## What happened

President Trump used a Thursday visit to Peterbilt's plant in Denton, Texas, to promote tariffs on imported medium- and heavy-duty trucks. He joked that he would buy and drive one of the trucks if he could get approval, saying the Secret Service had a problem with the idea. Reports from the event describe no new tariff, rule or deadline.

Plant numbers came from the company and the White House. Peterbilt said it has added more than 1,000 jobs at Denton, and the administration said PACCAR has added more than 2,000 jobs nationwide since the tariffs took effect. The "200 trucks a day" figure was Trump's retelling of a conversation. These numbers are claims made at a campaign-style event, not audited data.

## The tariff you are already paying for

The rule is Proclamation 10984. It put a 25% tariff on certain imported medium- and heavy-duty trucks and parts, effective November 1, 2025. The details decide who pays:

- Trucks that do not qualify for USMCA treatment are taxed on their full value. For those that qualify, the tariff applies only to the non-U.S. content.
- Covered parts include engines, transmissions, tires and chassis. USMCA-compliant parts are exempt until Commerce sets up a process to tax their non-U.S. content.
- Truck makers that assemble in the U.S. get an offset worth 3.75% of vehicle value through October 2030.

Commerce issued procedures in February for importers of USMCA-eligible trucks to document their U.S. content.

## What it costs you

No official figure exists for what the tariff adds to a truck. Estimates differ. One industry summary says the American Trucking Associations put the increase at up to $35,000 per truck, while ACT Research projects about $10,000 for 2026 because USMCA-compliant trucks are partly exempt. The actual hit depends on where your truck and its engine are built and how much the maker passes on.

## Emissions changes are still unsettled

Trump also credited rollbacks of Biden-era emissions rules. For the 2027 NOx rule, the situation is not final. The 0.035 g/bhp-hr limit is not changing. EPA is proposing changes to compliance requirements. The Federal Register proposal would keep the model year 2026 emissions warranty periods for 2027 and later engines. It would also end DEF derates for new vehicles. The comment period ran through August 29, 2026. Until EPA finalizes the rule, warranty terms on 2027 trucks may still change.

A separate fuel economy fight is in the news. The lawsuits filed today challenge lower standards for passenger cars and light trucks, not Class 8 tractors.

## What to do

- **Get quotes in writing.** Ask whether the price includes tariff costs or can be adjusted after you order.
- **Ask where the truck and engine are built** and whether the truck is USMCA-qualifying. That affects how much tariff is built into the price.
- **Ask about warranty terms** on any model year 2027 truck. Get the emissions warranty in writing, since the federal rule may still change.
- **Budget for parts.** Tariffs cover some parts, so repair bills could rise even if you never buy new.
- **Compare used.** Spend time on the used market if the new-truck math does not work. Have a lender pre-qualify you before you start shopping.
- **Ignore the headlines.** Nothing from the Denton event requires action this week.

**Bottom line:** The Peterbilt visit changed no rules, so judge any new-truck deal on its written price, build location and warranty terms.
