Buying a first truck is usually the biggest financial decision a new driver makes. The lender's checklist is fairly consistent, but the numbers vary a lot from lender to lender. Knowing what they look at lets you fix weak spots before you apply, not after a denial.

## What lenders look at

**Your credit.** Lenders use your credit history to judge how likely you are to repay. Credit score and the size of your down payment are among the main factors lenders weigh for approval. Minimum scores are set by each lender, so no single number applies everywhere.

**Your down payment.** Some lenders ask for as much as 30%, depending on your credit. Lenders also treat a first-time buyer with no business history as a higher risk. Plan for a larger share up front than a veteran owner would put down.

**The truck itself.** The truck is collateral for the loan, so the lender wants its details. It will not approve a loan if the truck is not worth enough to secure the debt. Age and mileage affect that value, and many lenders have their own limits on both.

**Time in business and cash flow.** A brand-new company has no revenue record. Expect questions about your driving experience, bank statements, and whether you have freight lined up.

**Your legal ability to operate.** Before a carrier starts interstate operations, it must register with FMCSA and get a USDOT number. For-hire carriers also need operating authority. Companies may need a USDOT number, an MC number, or both. Some lenders ask about authority status, so check your own situation first.

## Know the rate picture

Rates differ widely. One consumer finance publisher puts average semi-truck rates anywhere from 6% to 35% or higher. The same source says the lowest rates go to owners with excellent credit and more than two years in business. A first-time buyer should assume the quote will land higher than the best advertised numbers. No lender can promise a rate before reviewing your file.

## Compare the main options

- **Equipment loan.** You own the truck and the lender holds a lien until it is paid off.
- **Lease.** This is a long-term rental, and you generally do not own the truck at the end. Read the end-of-term terms closely.
- **SBA-backed loan.** The SBA guarantees part of a loan made by a participating lender. It guarantees up to 85% of loans of $150,000 or less, and up to 75% above that. Buying machinery and equipment is an eligible use. The term is generally 10 years or less, unless it finances equipment with a useful life over ten years. The borrower still has to be creditworthy and show a reasonable ability to repay. Approval can take longer than other routes.
- **Carrier lease-purchase programs.** These tie the truck to one carrier. Have someone who knows trucking contracts read the whole agreement before you sign.

## Budget beyond the payment

Lenders look at whether you can carry the truck, not only buy it. Budget for insurance, fuel, maintenance, and slow-pay stretches. Federal rules set a minimum of $750,000 in public liability coverage for for-hire carriers hauling nonhazardous property in interstate commerce with a GVWR of 10,001 pounds or more. Hazmat loads require higher limits. Your actual policy cost depends on your record, experience, and the insurer.

Tax treatment can also affect how you plan the purchase. Federal depreciation rules changed recently, and some states do not follow the federal rules. Ask a tax professional before relying on a write-off in your budget.

## What to do

1. Pull your credit reports and fix errors before applying. Avoid opening other credit lines in the months before.
2. Save your down payment and keep a cash reserve on top of it.
3. Gather your CDL, driving history, tax returns, and several months of bank statements.
4. Get insurance quotes for the exact truck before you commit.
5. Confirm your USDOT and authority needs, or the lease arrangement you will run under.
6. Get written quotes from more than one lender. Compare total cost, fees, term, and prepayment rules, not just the monthly payment.
7. Read every page, including late fees, default terms, and what happens to the truck if you fall behind.

## Common mistakes

- Shopping for the truck before knowing what you can be approved for.
- Focusing on the monthly payment and ignoring the length and total cost.
- Skipping a pre-purchase inspection on a used truck.
- Accepting a deal with no credit check or no down payment without checking the cost behind it.
- Signing a lease-purchase or financing deal under time pressure.

## Self-check questions

Test yourself before you apply. What is the total cost of this deal over the full term? Can I make payments for several months if freight slows down? What happens if I miss a payment? Who owns the truck, and when? If you cannot answer these, keep asking questions.

Rules and lender policies change. Check current terms with FMCSA, the SBA, your state, and the lender directly.

**Bottom line:** Lenders mainly want proof you can repay, so build your credit, down payment, and paperwork first, and compare total cost before you sign.
