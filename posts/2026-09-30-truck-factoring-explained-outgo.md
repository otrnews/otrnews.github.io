Brokers often take 30 days or longer to pay. Fuel, insurance, and the truck note don't wait. That gap is where a lot of small carriers get into trouble, and it's the problem factoring is built to solve.

## How factoring works

You deliver the load and send your invoice and paperwork to a factoring company instead of waiting on the broker. The factor pays you most or all of the invoice right away, keeps a percentage as its fee, and collects from the broker. You're trading a few percent of the load for cash now and for not chasing payments yourself.

- **Recourse factoring** means you owe the money back if the broker never pays.
- **Non-recourse factoring** means the factor takes that loss if the broker goes under, though the fine print on what counts varies.
- **Contracts** range from month-to-month to a year or longer, and some charge monthly minimums or fees to leave.

## Is factoring right for you?

Factoring usually makes sense if:

- You're on new authority with little cash cushion.
- You book most of your freight through brokers.
- Chasing payments is eating time you should spend driving.

You may not need it if:

- You have two months or more of expenses in the bank.
- Most of your freight is with direct shippers who pay quickly.
- You already have a back office handling billing and collections.

## Keep the math honest

On a $3,000 load, a 3% fee is $90. If that money covers fuel and keeps you moving instead of sitting for a week, it pays for itself. If it just lands in savings, you're paying for speed you didn't need.

**Bottom line:** Factoring is a cash-flow tool, not free money. Use it when getting paid fast keeps the truck rolling, and compare fees, recourse terms, and contracts before you sign.
