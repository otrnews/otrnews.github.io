Contract freight now pays better than spot freight again. A report released Thursday shows dry van contract rates ended August 22 cents a mile above spot. That is a switch from June, when spot was ahead. If you run mostly on load boards, that gap is the number to watch.

## What the report found

U.S. Bank reported that contract dry van rates ended August 22 cents a mile above spot freight, two months after spot briefly priced higher than contract. The figures come from the October edition of the U.S. Bank Freight Payment Index – Rates Edition, which the bank produces with DAT Freight & Analytics. This edition covers June through August.

The per-mile numbers, which are linehaul rates without fuel:

- **Spot:** $2.38 in June, $2.35 in July and $2.17 in August.
- **Contract:** $2.30 in June, $2.38 in July and $2.39 in August.

Average fuel surcharges rose from 62 cents a mile in June to 70 cents in August. So the all-in price shippers paid held up better than the linehaul rate did.

## Why spot slipped

The report's authors read the gap as a sign that shippers still value committed capacity, while spot freight absorbs more of the market's weakness. The same report says capacity is leaving the market faster than freight demand is falling.

Rates are still well above last year. Spot linehaul in August was up 35.6% from August 2025, when it averaged $1.60 a mile. A recent weekly DAT report put van linehaul up 32% year over year. The same report showed the van all-in spot rate at $3.01 a mile, up 5 cents. Each all-in price gain came from fuel.

## Diesel is doing the lifting

Fuel is the reason all-in numbers look healthy while linehaul sags. The EIA reported $6.285 a gallon for the week ending Sept. 14, the first weekly reading above $6 in its records going back to 2007. DAT's analysts also say diesel is trending higher into the fall.

For the driver, a higher all-in rate does not mean more money in your pocket. If fuel climbs 30 cents a gallon, the surcharge in the rate goes up only a few cents a mile. A truck getting 6.5 mpg burns about 4.6 cents a mile for each 30-cent rise in diesel (30 cents divided by 6.5 mpg). The extra surcharge, and the fuel cost you eat before it shows up, can leave you even or behind. DAT notes its all-in rates trail the fuel market by about a week by design. You pay at the pump today, but the surcharge reflects last week's price.

## What the numbers do not tell you

The index covers dry van only. It is built from shipper freight payments, so it shows what shippers pay and not what drivers take home. Your rate depends on lane, equipment, broker margin and whether you hold a direct contract. Flatbed and reefer follow their own patterns. One August data point also shows that DAT put the reefer gap at 4 cents a mile and the flatbed contract-spot gap at 38 cents.

## What to do

- **Price spot loads on linehaul, not all-in.** Strip out the fuel surcharge and compare the remaining number to the roughly $2.17 spot average. Broker offers that look fine all-in can be thin once fuel is removed.
- **Ask for a fuel surcharge schedule in writing** on any contract or dedicated lane. Know the base fuel price it starts from and how often it resets.
- **Look at contract or dedicated freight.** With contract paying 22 cents more at the market level, shippers' committed freight is where the premium sits. Small fleets and owner-operators can ask existing brokers and shippers about multi-week commitments.
- **Run your cost per mile at today's diesel price.** Many operating-cost sheets still use fuel numbers from the spring. At more than $6 diesel, update yours before you accept a lane.
- **Watch the weekly DAT and EIA numbers.** If linehaul spot rates start to rise, brokers will notice, and your leverage improves. DAT's 35-day forecast showed rates holding flat at around $2.20 a mile into mid-October. That was an earlier projection, and it can change.

## What comes next

The report does not say how long contract will hold its lead. With truck posts running about 26% below a year ago for van, capacity is tight, which supports rates. Spot softness since June suggests shippers still have leverage on the transactional side.

**Bottom line:** Contract freight is paying about 22 cents a mile more than spot, and diesel is inflating all-in spot numbers, so judge every load on linehaul and your real fuel cost.
