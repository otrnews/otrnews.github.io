For an owner-operator or small fleet, the load board is where the week gets made or lost. DAT One is the most widely used load board in North America. That matters for one reason: more brokers posting means more loads to choose from, and more choice means you can say no to freight that doesn't pay.

## What DAT gives you

- **Load search that fits how you run.** Search by origin, destination, equipment, and deadhead radius. Save searches for your regular lanes so new postings alert you on your phone.
- **Rate data before you call.** DAT shows what a lane has actually been paying, so you negotiate from the market instead of guessing.
- **Broker credit and days-to-pay.** Check how a broker scores and how long they take to pay before you haul for them. This one habit saves a lot of carriers from getting burned.
- **Post your truck.** List your available truck and let brokers call you, which helps on backhauls out of slow markets.
- **Factoring built in.** Loads marked eligible for Outgo factoring can be booked and funded inside the same platform. See our guide to [truck factoring](/news/truck-factoring-explained-outgo/).

## A routine that pays

### Know your cost per mile first
Add up fuel, insurance, truck payment, maintenance, permits, and what you need to pay yourself, then divide by the miles you run in a month. Any load under that number loses money, no matter how good it looks. Our free [cost per mile calculator](/tools/cost-per-mile/) does the math.

### Check the lane rate, then counter
Look up the recent rate for the lane before you call. If the posted rate is under the market, counter with a number and say where it comes from. Brokers expect it.

### Think in round trips
A strong rate into a weak market can leave you deadheading home empty. Check what freight looks like out of the delivery area before you book.

### Vet every broker
Check the credit score and days-to-pay, make sure the MC number matches the company you're talking to, and get the rate confirmation in writing before you roll. Double-brokering and fake-broker scams are common, and a phone number on a load post is not proof of who you're dealing with.

## Which plan do you need?

DAT sells several tiers, and not every plan includes rate data and broker credit. If you're booking most of your own freight, those two features usually pay for the difference. Check DAT's current plans before you sign up, because features and prices change.

**Bottom line:** Use DAT to know the market, not just to find loads. Carriers who check rates and vet brokers before every booking keep more of what they haul.
