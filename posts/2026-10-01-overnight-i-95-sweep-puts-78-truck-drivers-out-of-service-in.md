A single overnight sweep along the I-95 corridor last weekend put 78 truck drivers out of service. Federal and state agencies worked across four Southeastern states on Saturday, Sept. 26. The operation was called Operation Saturday Night Fever. If you run the East Coast at night, this is the kind of stop to expect more of.

## What happened

The operation covered highways in South Carolina, North Carolina, Florida and Georgia. FMCSA safety investigators worked with the Department of Homeland Security and state police to inspect trucks, verify driver credentials and look for safety violations.

FMCSA reported 552 inspections, with 78 drivers and 25 vehicles placed out of service. The South Carolina Department of Public Safety, however, put the inspection count at 568. The two agencies' totals for drivers and vehicles out of service match. Going by FMCSA's number, about 14 percent of inspections ended with a driver parked.

Other results came from state and local reports:

- Twelve of the out-of-service drivers were cited for English Language Proficiency (ELP) violations. The group also included operators holding non-domiciled CDLs.
- The four states issued 173 moving violations.
- Participating agencies logged seven criminal arrests and 11 detentions by U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement.

## What inspectors were looking for

The target list was broad. Inspectors checked driver credentials, hours-of-service compliance, electronic logging devices, distracted driving, cargo securement and English language proficiency. Florida troopers specifically focused on driver fatigue, logbook tampering, mechanical breakdowns, distracted or impaired driving, and unsecured cargo.

The timing was deliberate. South Carolina's State Transport Police called the sweep a periodic overnight initiative aimed at unsafe trucks and drivers in late-night and early morning hours. It was the third such operation by the agency in 2026, and the first to go beyond South Carolina by teaming up with FMCSA and partners in the other three states. South Carolina put 70 officers on its interstates for the effort.

FMCSA Administrator Derek Barrs said the operation showed the strength of law enforcement partnerships and a shared responsibility for road safety.

## What it means for drivers

Nothing in this sweep is a new rule. Hours of service, ELDs, cargo securement and CDL requirements were already in force. What changed is who showed up. Federal investigators, DHS and four state agencies ran the same checks on the same night, and the state that hosted the operation says it plans more of them. Parking in a rest area to dodge a state's weigh station does not help when the next state runs a matching sweep.

A drivers-out-of-service order also costs money. A parked truck means a missed delivery, possible detention or layover trouble with the broker, and a violation on your record that follows you. For an owner-operator, a night sidelined at the roadside comes out of your own pocket.

## What to do

- **Check your logs before you roll.** Make sure your ELD records are accurate and complete, and that you know how to pull up your records for an officer. Logbook tampering was on Florida's list.
- **Do the pre-trip and keep doing it.** Brakes, tires, lights and air lines were part of the 25 vehicle shutdowns. A walk-around takes a few minutes. A roadside out-of-service takes the rest of the night.
- **Re-check your load.** Unsecured cargo was an explicit focus. Look at straps, chains and tarps again after the first stretch of road.
- **Keep your paperwork in reach.** That means your CDL, medical card, registration and any endorsements. Drivers with non-domiciled CDLs were among those inspected, so confirm your license documents are current and correct.
- **Know the English language requirement.** Federal rules require drivers to be able to communicate with officers and understand traffic signs. Twelve drivers were shut down for it in one night.
- **Do not drive tired.** The operation was built around fatigue and late-night risk. If you are drowsy, stop. Pulling over is cheaper than a crash or a citation.
- **Keep your phone down.** Distracted driving was on the checklist.

## What is still unclear

FMCSA did not say how many of the 78 out-of-service drivers were shut down for each individual violation category beyond the ELP figure. It also has not announced when or where the next joint operation will happen. Earlier federal crackdowns have been much larger. One three-day operation across four Midwestern states ended in about 766 out-of-service orders and 86 driver arrests. That suggests the pace of multi-agency sweeps has picked up this year.

**Bottom line:** A sweep like this can land on any busy freight corridor on a weekend night, so keep your logs, truck, load and paperwork ready every trip.
