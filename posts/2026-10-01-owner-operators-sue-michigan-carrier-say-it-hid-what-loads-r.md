A group of owner-operators says a Michigan carrier showed them lower load values than customers actually paid, then took its cut from the smaller number. If you're paid a percentage of the load, that is a direct hit to your settlement. The claims are allegations only. They have not been tested in court.

## What the lawsuit says

The proposed class action was filed Sept. 17 in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan. The plaintiffs are Adis and Denisa Bajgoric, Sasa Susa (doing business as ASP Logistics), Mensud Topic, Edin Topic and George Ureche. They are suing Red Line Logistics, Zaim Bajgoric and Anel Penava. The case is *Bajgoric et al. v. Red Line Logistics Inc. et al.*, No. 2:26-cv-13533. The suit seeks more than $1 million in damages.

The complaint says Red Line got freight from outside customers and assigned the loads to owner-operators under exclusive lease agreements. Under those leases, drivers were supposed to get a percentage of load revenue, minus certain costs and expenses. One agreement cited in the complaint gave the owner-operator 80% of the load value and Red Line 20%.

The alleged trick is in the starting number. In one example, Red Line would keep a $2,500 difference and figure the driver's share from the lower amount. Here is what that would mean on a hypothetical load. A customer pays $5,000 and the driver is shown $2,500. At an 80% share, the driver gets $2,000 when the real share would be $4,000. That is an illustration, not a figure from the complaint.

Court records list the case under the racketeering category, and the docket gives breach of contract as the cause of action. The plaintiffs claim the carrier used electronic communications and separate freight-management systems in a yearslong scheme to hide load revenue.

## What is still unknown

The allegations have not been proven in court. As of the report, Red Line, Bajgoric and Penava had not filed a response. OTR News could not confirm any company statement. Treat this as a lawsuit that was just filed, not a finding.

## Why it matters to you

Similar disputes have ended in court before. A federal court once found that RSP Express shorted owner-operators by underreporting part of its gross revenue. The drivers won a judgment of more than $10.4 million. A different case, over alleged altered rate sheets, ended in a settlement. That filing did not say how much was paid.

If your pay is a straight percentage, you can't tell whether your share is right unless you know the real rate.

## What to do

- **Read your lease.** Federal truth-in-leasing rules say that when pay is a percentage of gross revenue, the lease must say the carrier will give you a copy of the rated freight bill before or at the time of settlement. The rated freight bill is the document showing what the customer was charged. Make sure your lease says this.
- **Ask for the paperwork on every load.** The lease must also let you examine the carrier's tariff, or for contract carriers the documents that rates and charges are computed from. The carrier may black out shipper and consignee names. Rates should not be blacked out.
- **Compare numbers.** Check the load amount on your dispatch sheet against the rate confirmation, the rated freight bill and your settlement. If you can see the load on a board or have a broker contact, check the posted rate.
- **Keep your own records.** Save every dispatch sheet, rate confirmation, settlement statement and text or email about a load. Dated copies are what you will need if you ever dispute a settlement.
- **Watch for stalling.** A carrier that won't hand over freight bills or similar documents puts you at a disadvantage in any pay dispute, as one trucking attorney points out.
- **Get advice before you sign or walk away.** If numbers don't match, talk to a transportation attorney or your owner-operator association. Note that Part 376 covers lease operators, not company drivers in the carrier's own trucks.

## Bottom line

**Bottom line:** The Red Line claims are unproven, but a percentage-pay lease only works if you can see what the customer paid, so ask for the rated freight bill on every load.
