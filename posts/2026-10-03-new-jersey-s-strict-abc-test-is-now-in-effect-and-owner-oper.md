New Jersey's independent contractor rules took effect Thursday, Oct. 1. Gov. Mikie Sherrill turned down requests to push them back. For owner-operators leased on to carriers that haul in New Jersey, the rules put the "ABC test" in writing, and they could affect how carriers treat 1099 drivers.

## What took effect

The state Department of Labor and Workforce Development adopted the regulations on May 5, 2026. They spell out how the state applies its ABC test. The test dates to the state's 1936 unemployment law, and its three prongs have not changed.

Under the test, a worker is an employee unless all three prongs are met. The prongs are:
- The worker is free from control or direction over the work.
- The work is outside the company's usual course of business, or is done away from all of its places of business.
- The worker is customarily engaged in an independently established trade or business.

The burden is on the hiring company. The business has to prove the worker qualifies. A written contract that calls someone an independent contractor does not settle the question by itself. That matters for lease agreements. A signed lease alone may not protect either side.

## Why the delay failed

The effective date had already moved once. The rules were first set for June 1, and the governor later granted a four-month delay. Senate President Nicholas Scutari asked for a further delay until July 1, 2027. The New Jersey Business & Industry Association and the state Chamber of Commerce asked for the same date.

Sherrill held firm. She said the rule writes existing court decisions and regulations into code, and that anyone who is an independent contractor today will still be one tomorrow. She also said she is willing to approve legislation exempting some professions from the test. No trucking exemption has been announced.

## What the trucking angle is

Critics say the rule could disrupt truckers. Coverage of the launch lists truckers among the groups critics say could be hit. FreightWaves reports the rule is a standard that a regulator or a court can use when deciding contractor status. It does not by itself reclassify anyone. Disputes would come through state agencies or lawsuits.

Federal law is also part of the picture. Carriers have argued that the federal law barring states from regulating motor carrier prices, routes and services should block state classification tests. In 2019 the Third Circuit ruled that this federal law does not preempt New Jersey's ABC test. That case, Bedoya v. American Eagle Express, involved delivery drivers. The court said the test does not stop trucking companies from hiring contractors. It only decides whether a driver really is one. OTR News found no confirmed new court challenge to the October rule as of Saturday.

## What to do

These steps are general guidance, not legal advice.

- **Look at your lease and how you actually work.** The test looks at real control. Check who sets your routes and schedule. Check who requires uniforms, branding or company procedures. A carrier that controls all of that has a harder time showing you are independent.
- **Document your business.** Keep records showing you run your own operation. Useful items include your own authority or a lease-on arrangement, equipment you own, insurance, and invoices to more than one customer if you have them.
- **Ask your carrier how it is handling New Jersey.** Some carriers may change contracts, pay structures or who they use for NJ freight. Get any change in writing before you sign.
- **Know what you may be giving up.** Employee status can bring wage-and-hour protections. It can also mean the carrier owns the truck costs and you stop running as a business. Decide which you want before a contract is rewritten for you.
- **Talk to a lawyer before signing anything new.** Do this if you are a small fleet with drivers on 1099s, or an owner-operator in a dispute over pay or deductions.
- **Watch Trenton.** Any exemption would come from the Legislature, and none has passed.

## What is still unclear

It is not yet known how aggressively the state labor department will enforce the rule against trucking companies. It is also unknown whether carriers will bring a new federal preemption challenge. Business groups say the rulemaking drew more than 9,500 comments and that about 99% were opposed, but that did not change the outcome.

**Bottom line:** New Jersey's ABC test is now the rule, the burden is on the company that hires you, and owner-operators in the state should check their contracts and records now.
