The Group of Seven nations agreed Friday to release up to 100 million barrels of diesel and crude oil from emergency reserves. Diesel is at record highs, so the move is aimed squarely at truckers. Analysts say it will ease pressure, but it won't bring pump prices back down quickly.

## What the G7 agreed to

The G7 statement describes a release coordinated through the International Energy Agency. It is to begin immediately and run over four months, with a frontloaded, substantial diesel release in the first 20 days. The leaders also said they would meet in the coming days to discuss possible additional diesel releases if needed.

After the call, President Trump said Europe had agreed to release a large amount of its heavily stocked diesel and that the process would begin immediately. The Trump administration had been pushing Europe to deploy its stocks as an alternative to a U.S. diesel export ban.

## Where diesel prices stand

The EIA's national average on-highway diesel price fell 15 cents to $6.382 in data released September 29. That is a small break from the peak. Diesel set an all-time record of $6.529 a gallon the week of September 21. AAA put the national average at $6.439, which is $2.760 higher than a year ago.

Prices vary a lot by region. One market report put the West Coast at $7.412 a gallon, about 16% above the national average.

## Why this won't fix prices overnight

Several things limit the relief:

- In March, IEA members agreed to release 400 million barrels. The new 100-million-barrel decision covers commitments already made. One energy outlet reads it the same way. It says the barrels help complete the March pledges rather than adding a clearly new batch.
- Emergency inventories can bridge a supply disruption, but the underlying shortage depends on getting damaged and idled refineries back in service.
- An industry analysis site reports Goldman Sachs estimates the release offsets only about half of the recent diesel price rise. OTR News could not confirm that estimate with Goldman directly.
- EIA expects the refiners' diesel margin, known as the crack spread, to stay above $2 a gallon through November before declining steadily through mid-2027. That margin is part of what drives retail prices.

## The export ban question is still open

On Sunday, Trump said the White House was considering a U.S. diesel export ban "very seriously." He said again on Wednesday that he remained open to it, while acknowledging it carries risks. Business groups including the Chamber of Commerce and the American Petroleum Institute warned in a letter that a ban would lead to less fuel production and rising costs for truckers, among others.

One analysis says the G7's pledge against export restrictions constrains a U.S. ban but does not eliminate the risk. That is analyst opinion, not an announced policy. No ban has been issued as of Friday.

## What to do

- **Check your fuel surcharge.** If you haul on contract or for a carrier, confirm how the surcharge is calculated and which index it follows. Diesel moved about 15 cents in one week, so a stale formula can cost you real money.
- **Price new loads at today's fuel cost.** Don't use last month's number when you quote a spot load or negotiate a rate. Work out cost per mile at $6.40 or higher, and add more if you run the West Coast.
- **Plan your fill-ups.** Use your fuel card discounts and a fuel app, and buy where the price is lowest along your route. Avoid filling in high-cost states when you can reach a cheaper one.
- **Look up state relief.** Some states are cutting diesel taxes. OTR News has reported that Ohio cut its tax to 1 cent for 90 days. Check whether the states on your lanes have done the same.
- **Cut idle time and slow down.** At these prices, every gallon wasted at idle or at high speed costs more than it did a year ago.
- **Don't count on a quick drop.** Keep your cash reserves where they are until prices actually fall at the pump.

## What to watch

Watch the weekly EIA diesel number, which comes out on Mondays. Also watch the IEA meeting the G7 mentioned, since it could add more diesel. And watch any White House decision on an export ban. The first 20 days of the frontloaded release will show whether supply improves.

**Bottom line:** The G7 release adds diesel to a tight market, but analysts say it mostly speeds up supply already promised, so budget for $6-plus diesel for now.
